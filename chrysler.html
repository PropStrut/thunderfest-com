CHRYSLER CORPORATION ANNOUNCES RENEWAL
OF GOLD CUP TITLE SPONSORSHIP THROUGH 2001.

(Detroit, MI) - The Spirit Of Detroit Thunderfest is pleased to announce that Chrysler Corporation has agreed to renew its title sponsorship of the Detroit APBA Gold Cup Hydroplane Races. The extension agreement makes Chrysler the sole title and presenting sponsor of Detroit's prestigious powerboat competition for 1999, 2000 and 2001.

The event is officially titled as The Chrysler Jeep Detroit APBA Gold Cup, presented by The Southeast Michigan Jeep Dealers and The Chrysler - Plymouth Superstores.

Chrysler's continued sponsorship of the Chrysler Jeep Gold Cup represents the company's on-going commitment to the City of Detroit and the Gold Cup race. "We're delighted to renew our title sponsorship agreement with this cherished Detroit civic event," said Arthur (Bud) Liebler, Vice President, Marketing for Chrysler Corporation. "The Gold Cup draws more than 500,000 spectators to the Detroit riverfront each year. It's a terrific way to promote Detroit and along with our southeastern Michigan Chrysler-Plymouth and Jeep dealers, we're happy to do whatever we can to make that happen."

"We are thrilled that Chrysler Corporation has elected to continue its sponsorship with us," said Thomas R. McInnes, Chairman of The Board of The Spirit of Detroit Thunderfest.

"Chrysler's continued commitment to the Chrysler Jeep Gold Cup is a direct reflection of the company's outstanding dedication to the City of Detroit," continued McInnes. "Our millions of race fans and the greater Detroit community are very excited by this long-term partnership."

The Chrysler Jeep Gold Cup is recognized as the country's largest single-day motorsports event. The world-renowned motorsports competition is also one of the City's oldest civic events, dating back to 1916 when the first Detroit Gold Cup race was held near Belle Isle. The Spirit Of Detroit Thunderfest is the all-volunteer, not-for-profit association that has staged the annual hydroplane race since 1962.

Chrysler Corporation shares a rich history with Gold Cup hydroplane racing in Detroit. Horace Dodge of the Dodge Motor Car Company was a significant powerboat owner in the 1930's, racing the DELPHINE, EXCUSE ME and MISS SYNDICATE hydroplanes. The DELPHINE IV won the 1932 Gold Cup. After Horace Dodge died, his wife Delphine and son Horace Jr. continued the family's racing involvment. The Dodge Memorial Pits and Pit Tower were built and deeded to the City of Detroit by the Dodge Family in 1966.
